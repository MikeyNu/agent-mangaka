# Drawing wind and wind magic

Establish what causes the air to move, what responds, and the chosen instant. A room draft, a deliberate push, a sustained flow and a vortex can all use the same line treatment. Give each its own spatial path and physical response. Confirm the script and project rules for visibility, palette, scale, power ownership and consequences.

## Start with the receiver

1. **Draw the scene at rest.** Place a fixed landmark and the object with a free part, such as a pinned sleeve and its hem. Trace the same object in its displaced position. Describe direction by the bend of the hem, the stream of departing dust or a prop displaced against a stable edge. A change between two panels can carry a very quiet breeze by itself.
2. **Draw the path on a separate planning layer.** Mark the origin, the receiver and the exit or turning point. Fit the path through actual space. Keep an obstruction meaningful. Air can be shown turning through a doorway by repeating a small disturbance beyond the opening. Remove planning arrows from the final drawing unless the brief calls for a diagram.
3. **Choose a stroke family.** Pull one long curved stroke from a finer entry into a broader turn and taper it away. A second partial stroke may echo part of the turn at a different spacing. Allow the surroundings to show between the marks. Vary line weight and reserve dry-brush accents for a rapid edge. On pale ground use a narrow dark accent. On dark ground a light stroke may suffice. In monochrome, test using open paper, broken contour and the displaced silhouette alone.
4. **Seat the current in depth.** In an orbit, draw the far turn behind the caster, then let a short near turn pass in front. Leave an opening for the hand, face or destination. For an axial vortex, rough in a few cross-sections and choose the nearest one. Change width and interval with perspective. An oblique view exposes the axis. A view down the axis reveals a hollow eye. Keep a person or fixed piece of architecture visible for scale.
5. **Select what the air carries.** Fabric bends from its attachment, hair separates in groups, and settled dust lifts first near its source. A solid prop retains its shape as it slides, rocks or turns. Show an object's intended motion using its base, rim or corner and a stationary reference. If the prop contains liquid, track its passive tilt, spill or settling separately. Add consequences only where the scene warrants them.

## Match the scale to the event

| Event | What sells the drawing | Carryover to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quiet draft or ventilation | A free edge turning toward an opening and a second, later response along the same route. Leave room for dialogue and acting. | Inlet, exit, room geography and areas that have cleared. |
| Controlled push or correction | A small pressure arc and a visible change in the receiving object's position or tilt. | Contact, travel, final orientation and any residual movement. |
| Released gust or redirected flow | A gap after release and a disturbance downstream. For redirection, a bend in the path and a local response after that bend. | Screen direction after a cut and the point where rotation or travel begins. |
| Sustained spiral or large vortex | An open centre, broken near and far turns, one stable landmark and a second motion showing rotation. | Axis, direction of turn, extent and whether the subject is planted or actually lifted. |
| Bodily or creature gesture | An identifiable casting pose or approved body's motion followed by the effect on a separate receiver. | Actual anatomy, timing, distance and the difference between a natural movement and a scripted spell. |

Keep the effect consistent with nearby materials. Draw clear gaps through moving air. Reserve foam and beads for actual liquid and black billows for actual smoke or an established power. Background motion lines may describe camera or body movement. Give a wind mark an in-world origin, a path or a responding object so it can be read as air.

## Draw release and recovery

Choose one telling change per panel. A cuff may begin to lift as a caster acts. A curtain across the room may move on the next beat, then settle after the current weakens. Let loose particles travel beyond an already quiet sleeve. After a camera reversal, preserve where the air travels in the room even if that direction flips on the page. When the current righted or shifted a prop, preserve its final orientation and any spill or displaced contents.

## Review at reading size

Hide wind strokes, glow, particles and lettering. Identify the cause, one material that responded, its direction and a stable reference. If the action disappears, stage a clearer receiver. If a vortex becomes a solid pipe, open the contour and let the background show through. If a strong gust reads as camera speed alone, restore a local disturbance. Then check the normal render at phone or print size, and inspect the material attachments and intersections at native size. Across adjacent panels verify direction, occlusion, scale and the lasting state.

In a panel packet, put the rise, hold, release or recovery in `moment`, the casting body and receiver pose in `acting`, the scale reference and open spaces in `composition`, and current path, line treatment, material response and persistent state in `visual_constraints`. Attribute each study to its drawing decision. Project sheets and script retain authority over names, cast and abilities.
