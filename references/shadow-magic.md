# Drawing shadow magic

Read the current project's rules before assigning a caster, ability, realm, palette or cost. When the brief supports a creature summon, disappearance or a constructed object, plan the change in the scene first. The reader should be able to tell which happened from two small unlettered drawings.

## Set the shadow in the room

Establish the light, the object that casts the ordinary shadow and the plane it falls on. A patch on a wall should turn at a corner or be interrupted by a nearer object. Give the magical shadow one discernible point of contact or intentional separation. Block the main shape in a dark value. Reserve openings around the controlling fingers, a creature's leg or a finished handle. On a dark background separate planes with a narrow gap, a charcoal facet or a short edge accent. Use the project's colours. Leave areas of black quiet, especially beside a face.

Pull a solid contour through the load-bearing parts of a creature or the functional edge of an object. At a dissolving margin, ease pressure into narrow hooked wisps or a few broken dry strokes. Choose one place where the hard edge changes to smoke. A uniformly soft cloud gives away little about the form inside it. Camouflage, a cast shadow, a projection, an independently moving creature and a spatial portal may share a dark value. Use the script to decide which is present.

## Draw the change the script calls for

| Ability | Establish | Reveal | Follow through |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Summon a creature | Set the caster and a stable landmark. Design the summoned silhouette with its own anatomy, scale and facing. | Lead with a part that can visibly cross the threshold. A paw can take weight on the lit floor while the rest of its leg still disappears into the dark. | Give the creature an independent action or reaction. Preserve its identifying proportions, number and position. The script determines its origin, disposition and departure. |
| Disappear into shadow | Show the caster, location and shadow boundary clearly enough that the missing figure can be recognised. | Merge the figure in stages. Choose a last readable edge such as a cuff, fingertip or heel, then allow full absence at the hidden beat. | Hold on the same landmark. If the next beat involves movement, establish the allowed route or transition from the project's rules. When reappearing, recover a recognisable part of the same model. |
| Form an object | Decide what the object is, its size, orientation and intended grip or support. Draw the finished silhouette as a thumbnail. | Gather dark material along a purposeful edge. Resolve a handle, opening, rim or working face while some loose shadow still trails behind. | Show the object bearing, being carried or turning as the script requires. Keep the proportions and contact coherent until a scripted break or dissolution. |
| Screen or project a silhouette | Place the person or object doing the obscuring and the observer whose view changes. | Keep the shadow on its receiving plane, or visibly intercept the observer's sightline with the magical screen. | Restore the sightline when the cover clears. Keep an obscured person or projected likeness accounted for in the scene. |

## Preserve geography, value and identity

When using a fixed view, hold a doorjamb, paving seam or other rigid edge across neighbouring panels. This lets the reader see whether a person has vanished, a creature has arrived, or only the lighting has changed. Darken selectively. Keep an unlit area distinct from bent geometry or a changed viewpoint where the story treats those as separate effects. Use narrow light accents to clarify facing or overlap. A whole room need not glow for one small edge to read.

For a group, stage two distinct head heights or body orientations and count creatures across cuts. For a close shot, use contrast under a foot, at a jaw hinge or at a finger crossing a rim. Give the cast and any summoned subject their own identity references. A reference showing a creature, costume, ritual or coloured eyes establishes technique only unless the project separately adopts it.

## Review the unlettered sequence

Hide aura, lettering, wisps and eye lights. At phone size ask what entered, who vanished or what can now be held. For a summon that reads as a mural, cross the shadow edge and add independent motion or support. For a disappearance that reads as a change of scene, repeat the landmark and the exact lit edge. If the hidden caster is still traceable, remove the leftover rim only for the fully hidden beat. For a construct that reads as smoke, firm up one defining contour and the hand or surface that receives it.

In the panel packet, put the instant in `moment`, independent poses and hand contact in `acting`, the boundary and size anchor in `composition`, and the silhouette, edge transitions, observers' visibility and carryover in `visual_constraints`. Record further travel, summon behaviour and construct properties only to the extent that the current story establishes them.
