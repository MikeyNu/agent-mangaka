# Drawing lightning and electrical magic

Read the project rules before choosing the colour, caster and capabilities of the effect. Decide whether the panel shows a diagnostic reading, gathered static, a discharge in flight, contact, or the return to normal illumination. Decide who is acting and what changes as a result. A bright zigzag can represent any of these if the panel lacks a source or scale.

## Draw the main channel before its branches

1. **Establish source and destination.** Block the caster, limb, prop or surface. Leave a visible gap for a small static jump. For a large discharge, mark its intended route through the scene and locate the receiver or its off-panel direction. Decide which marks are behind or in front of each figure. Contact, near miss and a charge held in place require different endpoints.
2. **Design an asymmetric stroke.** Sketch one travelling line with a long run, a sharp elbow and a shorter offset. Widen a strong turn with a second, tapered contour. Let one narrower branch peel from a bend and end early. Leave a clear wedge between its tip and the main channel. Alternate shorter and longer intervals. A strong graphic bolt may have a few broad flats. A distant fork can become a hairline. At small reading sizes, simplify a static tick to one or two clear turns.
3. **Draw the form at the correct scale.** For a hand, preserve the spaces between fingers and wrap at most the necessary part of a wrist. For a body, concentrate arcs around the part doing the work and leave at least one clean silhouette. For a large flash, keep a wall, figure or doorway as a ruler. A foreground channel can be wider, and a far fork can disappear behind a landmark.
4. **Place the light.** Keep the project's element hue visible on the strongest recognisable bend. Reserve the lightest value for a core, fork or contact that needs emphasis. Put one short reflection on a facing material, then check the adjacent shadow. A shiny fitting, a matte sleeve and a finger pad can receive three differently shaped accents from the same event. Preserve skin tone and costume design across exposure changes. In monochrome, pair a sliver of reserved paper with a dark neighbouring plane and a taper at the branch.
5. **Remove the decoration as a test.** Hide bloom, lettering and loose particles. At reading size trace the channel from origin to destination, or locate the span between two fingertips. If the route breaks under a crossing arm, check the overlap in perspective. If branches read like a fence, remove alternate forks and vary lengths. If a white mass swallows the scene, reclaim one dark gap at the decisive bend.

## Stage time across panels

| Beat | Main drawing decision | Review |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Local static or reading | Use a short irregular jump across a meaningful gap, or a few small ticks on the identified prop. | The object keeps its shape and the slight change is visible against its own background. |
| Gather or hold | Draw the hand and receiving planes first. Put local arcs in a few clear gaps. | Small branches can change shape while the source stays fixed. |
| Release | Select a single path and foreshorten it in the scene. Make the leading channel readable before filling in shorter forks. | Origin, receiver or direction, source-facing highlights and the exact moment of contact. |
| Contact and flash | Anchor a compact flare to the receiving surface. Raise exposure locally or briefly as the scene requires. | Surface orientation, cast expression and the outcome specified by the script. |
| Recovery | Clear the main channel, restore ambient light, and show the changed object or character if the script calls for one. | Keep any grounded consequence across cuts. Decide separately whether sparks persist, briefly pulse or disappear. |

In a sustained effect redraw some secondary forks while retaining the endpoints. A held image of the same bright zigzag can suggest a solid object. Separate a magical pose, illumination changes, motion streaks and actual movement. Source pictures may contain glowing eyes, animal-shaped lightning, weapon shapes, flight or new stages of power. Use an individual feature only when a script or approved design establishes it.

For a panel packet, put the beat in `moment`, the hands and pose in `acting`, the legible route and scale anchor in `composition`, and the main channel, colour, branch hierarchy, local spill and after-state in `visual_constraints`. Assign each external study to the drawing choice it illustrates.
