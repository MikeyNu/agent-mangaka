# Drawing motion in action panels

Use this guide when a pose, a change of speed, or an exchange must read in still art. Work from the current script, character proportions, environment and approved drawing treatment. Select only the cues the reader needs for this beat. Carry the same drawing decisions into full colour, limited colour or monochrome using the project's own palette and line colour.

## Choose the instant

Read across the cut. Identify what the reader already knows, what moves relative to what, and what new fact the next panel must establish. Mark the starting position, path, selected instant and end position on a thumbnail. These are planning marks. Draw only the selected instant in the finished panel unless a temporal composite serves the brief.

| If the reader needs to feel | Select an instant and a visible comparison |
|---|---|
| Anticipation or a deliberate hold | Show gathered weight or a held gaze with open space in the direction of expectation. In a second, similarly framed beat, let one small feature move while the rest holds. Preserve surprise when the script withholds the approach. |
| A fast crossing or near miss | Establish the gap, then select a pose already clearing it. Give the reader a fixed edge to measure displacement. Keep a sliver of clean space between the passing silhouettes where separation matters. |
| A sudden contact | Choose the instant when the two contours meet and one form visibly yields, or cut from approach to the first clear consequence. Give contact one precise location. A single interrupted contour or displaced fold can carry the beat at close range. |
| Weight, resistance or a hard stop | Show what carries the load. Compress the silhouette over its support, let a loose edge continue moving after the torso stops, and hold long enough to see it settle. A fast heavy body can have a short approach on the page and a long recovery. |
| A reversal | Preserve the old direction in a trailing shape while the main mass starts a new path. If both directions compete, give the change of direction its own panel. |

## Construct the pose before the marks

Thumbnail each participant as an identifiable silhouette. Give the leading mass a line of action through the torso. Tip the shoulder and hip masses to show twist, stretch the open side, and compress the folded side. Decide which part bears weight, which is free and which lags. Account for the project's anatomy, including nonhuman proportions. Follow a moving extremity back through its overlaps to its owner. A large shape disappearing behind another needs a credible place to reappear.

Draw the other participant's timing as carefully as the mover's. Their gaze may still follow the old position, their clothing may just begin to shift, or their weight may already be displaced, depending on the chosen instant. For a planted pose, show a contact patch against the ground. For an airborne pose, use separation from the plane and an established height cue when available. Preserve a wedge of background around the decisive joint, gap or expression. If the action reads as two people posing independently, change the silhouettes or their spacing before adding ink.

## Choose a viewpoint that answers the beat

| Visual question | Camera decision | What makes the drawing hold together |
|---|---|---|
| How much distance closed, or did they pass each other? | Use a side or three-quarter wide view with both trajectories visible. | Establish screen direction and an identifiable ground feature. For contact, show a meeting edge and a response. If two forms merely cross in projection, reveal the gap in depth from this view or the next. |
| What is about to enter the reader's space? | Move the viewpoint close to the approaching form along an oblique path. | Enlarge the nearest plane, overlap it over a shorter visible middle segment, and reduce the far mass. Leave a slice of the connecting limb, face or other identity cue. Scaling the endpoint alone creates a swollen extremity. |
| How far above or below is the other figure? | Use a steep view past a cropped foreground mass toward the distant participant. | Establish one shared ground plane with a floor seam, size reference or grounded shadow appropriate to the light. Let empty distance build anticipation. Save actual contact for its scripted instant. |
| What changed at the instant of impact? | Crop tightly enough to see a local deformation or reaction. | Include a recognizable edge from each interacting form. If the crop hides ownership, establish it in the adjacent wider view. Return to enough space to read the consequence. |

Pick the simplest view that reveals the missing information. A tilted horizon can express disorientation. Keep gravity, projected floor and cast consistent with the map. At a side change, show the crossing or re-establish the positions before relying on the new screen direction. For a page, follow entry, contact and exit in the declared LTR or RTL order. For a scroll, preview consecutive phone-height windows and choose when the endpoint enters view. Reframing or gutter spacing can hold back a reveal. Never mirror character asymmetry to repair reading order.

Vary the duration of attention. A tight contact insert can interrupt two similarly framed wides, so the reader sees the distance gained against the same background. A second nearly identical panel can prolong a moment when only a cuff or a shadow has shifted. Spend the largest panel on the change the reader needs to feel most. Sometimes that is the empty space after the encounter.

## Place hand-drawn strokes by function

In the thumbnail, trace the path from its departing edge to its destination, then remove that guide. Pick the smallest family of strokes that supplies the missing information. State which edge stays sharp, where the marks begin and end, what they pass behind, and which area stays quiet.

| Mark | Placement and touch |
|---|---|
| Straight travel strokes | Pull a few strokes along a shared direction behind the departing contour. Let a sharp leading contour locate the present instant. Cluster lengths unevenly with gaps between groups. For a tracking view, keep the moving subject comparatively crisp and drag selected background edges opposite the subject's travel. |
| Turning or sweeping arc | Sketch the path around its pivot in the pictured plane first. A circle viewed obliquely narrows to an ellipse. Let a few tapered fragments follow the same turn. Break the arc where a nearer form occludes it and release it into clear space. A continuous band can read as a physical ribbon. |
| Broken moving contour | Replace a short section of the trailing outline with strokes pulled along the travel direction. Keep enough of the leading edge and a joint landmark to recover the shape. Use this local smear for a motion too quick to need another complete pose. |
| Impact accents | Place a small irregular group of short, pressure-weighted strokes beside the actual meeting point. Leave the meeting edges readable, or establish the result in the adjacent panel if the contact is intentionally hidden. For a miss, protect the air gap. A burst there would change the story. |
| Focus lines | Lead the eye inward from selected panel edges. Stop short of the face, gap or detail being emphasized. Focus lines can intensify a completely still subject. Their convergence describes attention and need not coincide with the scene's perspective vanishing point. |
| Form hatching | Wrap short strokes around the turning plane or gather them under an overlap to anchor volume. Keep them attached to the surface. Reserve the open air for marks that describe travel. |

Describe the hand as well as the path. For a pencilled or pressure-inked treatment, seat a firmer stroke at a useful overlap, pull through the curve, then lift to a fine or broken tip. Allow a dry skip on a trailing edge if it belongs to the approved brush. Vary starting points, lengths, spacing and pressure within a group while preserving its shared direction. Keep the decisive silhouette clean beside a rough trail. Uniform parallel rails, evenly spaced spokes and the same scratch texture on every surface flatten those distinctions. In colour, pick a mark value that separates from the local fill at reading size. Preserve the project's light, shadow shapes and materials.

Keep marks drawn for the reader separate from marks left in the world. A motion stroke ends with the beat. A scuff that appears on a surface needs a cause and a place in the next view. Use a fixed landmark to show displacement when the script or material gives no reason for damage.

## Show several instants only when needed

Choose a single pose when gesture and secondary motion carry the event. Use a partial afterimage when the reader needs the path of one moving part. Keep one current endpoint and one owner, then simplify earlier samples to broken contours with less internal detail. Distribute the samples along the actual projected path. For equal time intervals, widening gaps imply acceleration and closing gaps imply a stop. Leave a stationary anchor intact.

Use successive full silhouettes in one panel only when the route through space matters. Give them a shared landmark, consistent identifying shapes and a clear order across the reading path. Separate phases by placement, overlap and edge detail. A constant-opacity row of identical bodies can look like a crowd. For a temporal composite, use `representation_mode: temporal_montage`, bind repeated samples to the same character with `instances` of kind `temporal` and a named `purpose`, and list each physical participant once. Apply the actual consequence once. If readers still count extra people or impacts, split the phases across panels.

## Direct the generator

Write the script event and chosen instant in `moment`. Put support, leading mass and the response in each participant's `acting.pose`. Use `camera` and `composition` for projection, focal point and crop. Put the decisive visible relationship, brush behaviour, trail boundaries and protected area into the panel's `visual_constraints`. Include any spatial constraints already required there. These instructions travel through `packet`; a note left only in a thumbnail or a review ledger will not direct the image model.

For example, a brief might call for two figures passing on different depth planes. A usable direction is *“Keep the near sleeve crisp where it passes the far shoulder. Leave a strip of background between them. Pull three uneven, tapering marks off the sleeve's trailing seam. End them before they cross the other face.”* If the script changes that beat to a slow pass, the same camera can work with a small shift against the background and the sleeve hanging almost vertically. Write fresh directions from each brief. Reuse neither the example composition nor its stroke count by default.

## Read the result and repair it

1. Read the unlettered panel and its neighbours at delivery size. Identify who moved, from where to where, what touched or missed, and what changed. Check whether the movement feels fast, held or heavy at the intended point. Compare that reading with the script.
2. Ignore the trails and read the silhouette. If the body floats or the event disappears, repair the support, overlap, depth anchor or camera first. If the pose reads but the path points backward, repair just the trail on its departing edge.
3. Inspect the native detail at the leading contour, joins and stroke tips. Check the identity and anatomical side of each solid limb. A trail that looks like a second forearm needs reduced interior detail or a visible break from the joint. A mechanically repeated comb needs changes in grouping and pressure localized to that mark family.
4. Recheck the placed crop in the actual page or scroll. A gutter, balloon reserve or dark fill can close the decisive gap, hide the contact patch or make a temporal echo solid. Preserve cast, palette, physical outcome and adjacent continuity during repair.
