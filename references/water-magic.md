# Drawing water and water magic

Decide what the liquid does at this instant. Use the script to identify its source, who controls it, the receiver if there is one, and its state in the next panel. Use the project's established palette, illumination and limits on magic. An affinity display, a prop being moved by another power and a character shaping water each have different causes.

## Construct the liquid

1. **Draw the gesture and volume.** Mark a centreline from source to leading mass. Draw two or three cross-sections only where the stream flattens, narrows or turns toward the camera. Connect them with unequal curves. A suspended ball can start with a compact mass, a turning wave with a heavy belly and a lip, and a sweep with a broad thin sheet. Mark the width against a finger, a body or a landmark.
2. **Fold the edge.** Extend the leading lip and bring it around once. Leave a wedge of visible background inside that turn. Draw the nearer edge across the farther surface and interrupt the hidden line. Thicken the ink where two layers overlap and lift the stroke as it reaches the pale rim. Large uninterrupted colour or tone shapes carry the mass at reading size.
3. **Break the sheet selectively.** On a stretching or breaking edge, pull a skinny neck to a rounded bead, then draw a detached drop a little farther along its path. Vary the sizes and spacing. Short spray follows the local turn, liberated drops begin to fall and a quiet held mass can have a clean edge. Use foam at the curling or agitated part and keep enough open area to show what the foam sits on.
4. **Describe the surface.** Lay a darker fold, a broad mid-value and a few broken light reflections wrapping with the form. Heavy overlap can lose background detail. A thin region can preserve a displaced fragment of a background shape. Choose highlights from the environment. Self-illumination and local spill require a lighting or spell cue. In monochrome use paper gaps at the rim, a darker underside and restrained tone on the near plane. Test the silhouette and depth with all glow and particles hidden.
5. **Integrate the subject.** Draw the complete hand or prop before putting water in front of it. Reserve either a clear hover gap or an identifiable contact. A near stream may cross the wrist while its far continuation disappears behind it. Protect the important expression or gesture and the establishing landmark.

## Choose the panel beat

| Beat | One stroke or shape that sells it | Detail to carry forward |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gather and hold | Bring a narrow feed into a compact mass. Wrap a partial current across the surface and release its end into open space. | Which hand is in control, whether the source stays connected and the volume relative to the palm. |
| Sweep or project | Turn a ribbon so a wide face becomes a narrow edge. For a jet, establish the near cross-section and taper or foreshorten it along its path. | Path, screen direction, moment of separation and any uninterrupted background in a near miss. |
| Rotate or crest | Build the hollow first. The near rim crosses the far turn, and foam bunches where the lip starts to fall. | Axis of rotation, opening and whether the mass still holds its height. |
| Land and disperse | Flatten the leading volume at the receiving surface. Turn the edge outward into a fan and split its thinnest parts into drops. | Surface orientation, diminishing height, any widening ring, runoff or wet patch. |
| Form a likeness | Construct the larger silhouette out of connected currents, with at least one open interval and an underside. | Record whether the shape is figurative, a physical construct or a living subject. Use the story and approved design for identity or abilities. |

## Show time and scale

Choose one instant per panel. A lift starts with a neck at the source, bulges as it gathers, and leaves an air gap after release. A crest folds forward while its base carries the mass. A return has contact first, airborne drops next, slower ripples later. A controlled suspension may hold against gravity. Show which part is held and which drops have come free. Use a common anchor across scale changes. A wide sheet can gather into a narrower, thicker form. If the amount increases, give the reader a visible feed or an established story cause. A free-floating effect alone establishes no surface damage or wet clothing.

## Inspect the drawing

At thumbnail size, point to the origin, leading edge, widest mass, and one gap or overlap. Turn off all glow, surface sparkles and lettering. If only a blue disc remains, give one edge an overlap, a change of depth and a free drop. If it reads as a flame, interrupt the repeated pointed tongues with a folding sheet or rounded released beads. If it reads as a transparent solid, loosen a contour or shift a reflection in the next instant. If it reads as wind, give the ribbon thickness and weight. Inspect the hand, face and interrupted background at native size. In the next panel check the quantity, position and residual wetness against the script.
