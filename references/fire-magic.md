# Drawing fire and fire magic

Use the project's element rules and the current scene. Identify where the fire appears, what holds or drives it, its extent relative to the subject, and which instant the reader sees. Separate a flame in the world from an emblem, diagnostic light or symbolic background. Give each the appearance established by the script.

## Draw one readable flame

1. **Place the source and the gesture.** Lightly draw the hand, mouth, object or burning surface through the proposed effect. Mark the visible gap if the flame hovers. Sweep a single line from the broad living body toward the longest tip. A quiet flame can rise and lean. A swept flame follows the moving source, while its loose tips lag or curl away. A released mass has a leading end and a wake.
2. **Block the silhouette at reading size.** Draw a broad belly, a narrowing neck and an unequal pair of tips. Use a long convex sweep on one side. Cut a deeper curved notch into the other and lift the stroke to a fine hook. Vary these shapes with the motion. Leave a piece of background between adjacent tongues. In a small panel, one strong tongue and a detached fleck can be enough.
3. **Draw the light within that shape.** Give the main body a clear local colour or value. Draw a brighter interior that fattens at a pocket of light and runs out before some of the outer tips. Let it meet the outer shape on a few bright edges. Leave room for half-tones at the boundary. A stack of equally inset outlines makes the fire look laminated. In monochrome, use reserved paper, a darker neighbouring value and selective broken contours to preserve the same structure.
4. **Choose the marks by surface.** Pull a smooth pressure-varied curve at the broad turn, then a short lifted taper at the tip. A fast wake can drag into two or three unequal dry marks aligned with travel. Leave the brightest interior quiet. Put the descriptive hatching on the fingers, sleeve or stone receiving the light. Use a thin coloured edge where two flame masses would fuse. A sharp colour boundary can carry a silhouette on its own.
5. **Place the light in the scene.** Identify one plane that faces the fire and one that is occluded. For a flame above a palm, the inward faces of the fingers can catch it while the palm crease or back of a knuckle stays dark. A face across the room needs less spill than the nearby hand. Match reflected light to the surface finish and existing illumination. Check the silhouette first with the glow layer hidden.

## Choose the silhouette for the beat

| Intended drawing | Visible construction | Preserve at the final crop |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Held or hovering flame | One compact root or air gap, a broad body and a rising or wind-bent tongue. | The supporting gesture and a light-facing finger plane. |
| Wrapped limb or mantle | Let a near tongue cross part of the form. Send a second tongue behind its edge and reintroduce it on the far side. | Fingers, face or costume landmarks. A magical aura can overlap clothing without implying it is burning. |
| Gathered orb | Suggest volume with curved interior bands of changing width, incomplete arcs and a few escaping tongues. | The hand-to-orb gap, a quiet interval inside the swirl and the last attached or first released instant. |
| Sweep or travelling mass | For a sweep, place a broad connected band on the arc. For a released shape, separate it from the emitter and draw a shorter, narrowing wake. | Its owner, travel direction and a contour clear of the subject. |
| Directed stream or breath | Establish the emitter angle, then draw a broadening or foreshortened envelope. Open a few long channels in the flow. Let a contact plume turn against a visible receiving plane if the script calls for contact. | The hand or jaw, enough intervening space to read the route, and the scripted reach. |
| Enveloping or very large fire | Group the fire into distinct sheets. Alternate pale openings with a dark subject or known environmental landmark. | At least one size comparison, the focal expression or gesture, and a place where the effect ends or leaves the frame. |

These are staging options. Cast, affinity, forms, colours and consequences come from the current work. An animal-shaped silhouette, transformation, burning costume, persistent scorch or secondary ignition requires a story or approved design cue.

## Draw time across panels

For a sustained flame, anchor the source while the contour changes. Let a lower bulge travel upward, narrow behind it, divide at the tip and shed a fleck. Start the next bulge at a different height. For a release, carry a recognizable curve into the next panel as the air gap opens. For extinction, shorten the connected body and reduce nearby spill. Let any stray ember, smoke or surface change follow the established scene. Reuse the same floor or figure for scale when the effect grows. Use one telling instant for a single illustration.

## Check the unlettered drawing

At phone or print size, point to the source, widest connected mass, direction and a clear overlap or gap. Hide bloom, particles and SFX. If the result reads as a glowing ball, reopen part of the silhouette or add an escaping tongue and an asymmetric inner turn. If it reads as lightning, inspect whether branching linework has replaced the flame's body. If it reads as hair, vary the spacing and let a tip detach. Lower spill where it erases the cast's skin, expression or material. In a close crop, inspect the hand or jaw against its sheet and trace the contact in the following view.

In a panel packet, put the current instant in `moment`, comparative size and overlaps in `composition`, and the contour, palette, receiving light, protected gaps and carryover in `visual_constraints`. Choose a source study for a single visible principle and keep the project's approved identity and treatment in the reference set.
