## Purpose and evidence

Use this reference when converting a script and approved character/environment resources into individual panel briefs. It supplements the spatial continuity plan and the panel-generation workflow in [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md). The production rules below are a synthesis of artist instruction, primary research and the skill’s historical visual study. They are decisions to apply and test, not a guarantee of model fidelity.

The output remains 2D drawn art. A theoretical 3D scene map is a planning aid: it describes positions, heights, occlusion and camera direction without requiring 3D rendering. A flattened reference image does not reveal whether its original artist used models, tracing, photography, particular layers or particular software. Describe visible appearance and distinguish interpretation from proven production history.

Preserve the new project's setting. Separate reusable techniques such as anticipation, foreshortening and reaction inserts from costumes, architecture, technology, weapons and magic found in study material. A modern argument may use the same blocking as a fantasy courtyard exchange while taking every visible asset from the modern project manifest.

## Plan the reader's experience

For each scene, write the change the reader must understand: new information, a relationship shift, a decision, a physical event or an emotional turn. Allocate panels to these changes before producing detailed art. Every panel needs a purpose and a clear relationship to its neighbors; a visually impressive image that obscures the event is a failed panel.

Produce a compact beat table:

| Planning question | Required answer |
|---|---|
| Who acts, speaks or thinks? | Stable character IDs and exact utterance IDs |
| What is happening now? | One selected instant or an explicitly declared composite action |
| What changed since the previous beat? | Position, attention, costume, prop, injury or knowledge change |
| What should the reader notice first? | Named focal subject or action |
| What must remain visible? | Essential face, hand, contact, plot prop or environmental anchor |
| What happens outside this panel? | Explicit ellipsis or off-panel event, with its resulting state |
| What kind of image is this? | Literal scene, memory, hypothetical, replay, diagram or comic exaggeration |

Steve Ellis recommends thumbnailing the progression and deciding panel emphasis and flow before detailed drawing. Treat panel size, inset placement and scene endpoints as storytelling decisions. [Steve Ellis, comic and manga layouts](https://www.clipstudio.net/how-to-draw/archives/160963), accessed 2026-11-20.

Do not automatically convert each sentence into one panel. A silent reaction can deserve a panel; several short lines can share one. Do not fabricate decisive story actions to solve an attractive composition. If an ambiguity changes identity, causality or the intended outcome, resolve it before committing that beat.

## Construct the view before rendering

Start with the scene map. For a multi-character shot, locate all participants, major landmarks, exits, important props, floor levels and light sources. Then choose the camera. Changing the view changes the projection. Preserve the recorded positions of subjects and landmarks.

Describe camera position and orientation concretely: height, distance, viewing direction, elevation, tilt, framing and which surfaces should be visible. Record the foreground, middle distance and background. Use the spatial continuity reference for the active conversation or movement axis and justified axis changes.

Perspective rulers formalize one-, two- and three-point construction. Ground-parallel directions share the relevant horizon; looking steeply up or down introduces vertical convergence. Use this to test architecture, floors and bodies against one viewpoint rather than adding arbitrary diagonal lines. [CELSYS, perspective fundamentals](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/807), accessed 2026-11-20.

Camera distance controls perspective relationships. Focal length or field of view controls framing from that position. A close hand looks large because it is close relative to the face, not because a lens number magically enlarges it. For drawn prompts, pair any lens shorthand with the intended visible geometry; do not claim an exact focal length can be recovered from a stylized drawing. [Fujifilm, perspective and lens compression](https://www.fujifilm-x.com/en-gb/learning-centre/understanding-lens-compression/), accessed 2026-11-20.

| Shot choice | Use when | Protect from failure |
|---|---|---|
| Overhead or elevated overview | Geography, group changes, exits, important ground props | Actor spacing, feet, ground plane and landmark identity |
| Wide action view | The reader must understand a mechanism or displacement | Attacker, target, support, route and consequence |
| Medium interaction | Gesture and dialogue both matter | Hands, gaze targets and room for lettering |
| Over-shoulder or profile pair | Relationship and conversational direction matter | Correct foreground identity and reciprocal eyelines |
| Tight face or eye insert | A small reaction changes the scene | Enough identity cues and a legible surrounding sequence |
| Prop or hand insert | A signal, grip, clue or contact causes the next event | Correct owner, object state and orientation |
| Low or high viewpoint | A motivated power or vulnerability beat | Actual scale must remain stable in later neutral views |

For foreshortened limbs, check attachment at shoulder/hip, overlap order, joint direction, near/far scale and the hand or foot endpoint. Draw a simple pose silhouette or annotated sketch first when the camera is difficult. A reference pose is a guide to mechanics, not authority to change the character's anatomy or clothing.

## Compose for attention

Assign a primary focal point, a secondary response and an exit toward the next reading unit. Use value separation, silhouette, scale, overlap, direction of gaze, gesture, color contrast, edge clarity and controlled detail to support that hierarchy. Reserve lettering zones in the thumbnail so the final balloon does not compete with the decisive hand or face.

At thumbnail size, test whether the intended subject wins against architecture, jewelry, effects and text. At final reading size, test whether the reader can understand the action without zooming. An attractive full-resolution closeup cannot compensate for an unreadable published panel.

Treat thirds, diagonals, triangles and Fibonacci or golden-ratio guides as optional compositional scaffolds. Do not award a composition an automatic pass because a face sits on a ratio line. James Gurney's small eye-tracking demonstration found strong attention to depicted subjects and faces, and cautioned against assuming geometric grids dictate gaze. It is useful counterevidence to a formula, not proof of one universal viewing rule. [Gurney, Eye Tracking and Composition, Part 3](https://gurneyjourney.blogspot.com/2009/09/eye-tracking-and-composition-part-3.html), accessed 2026-11-20.

A borderless figure, slanted panel, asymmetrical inset or deliberate breakout can serve the beat. Specify that choice before generation, including what may cross the border and what must stay clipped. Avoid making every panel equally extreme; contrast gives an exceptional shot its emphasis.

## Direct acting and expression

For each visible actor, record internal emotion, outward display, intensity, intent, gaze target and physical behavior. The listener needs direction too. Anger might appear as a shout, contained stillness, a false smile or a defensive joke, depending on the script. Do not permit an unexplained cheerful expression in a painful argument; equally, do not erase motivated sarcasm or social masking.

Translate the performance into observable cues. Select the relevant combination of brow angle, eyelid tension, pupil direction, mouth shape, jaw tension, head angle, shoulders, torso, hands and weight distribution. Avoid relying on a generic emotion label alone. Magda Proski's facial construction lesson emphasizes how moving features act on the dimensional head, which helps preserve likeness during strong expression. [How to Draw Expressive Faces](https://www.clipstudio.net/how-to-draw/archives/157239), accessed 2026-11-20.

Gesture should carry the thought before costume detail is added. Ask what the person just did, is doing and is about to do. A hunched torso, protective arm and braced knee communicate a different condition from the same face on an upright relaxed body. [Stan Prokopenko, How to Draw Gesture](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-draw-gesture), accessed 2026-11-20.

Check the individual identity bundle against the correct sheet: facial proportions, hair silhouette and parting, age, body build, clothing construction, distinctive marks and accessories. Hair or eye color alone is inadequate when the cast shares those features. Evaluate back views against back-view references where available; the face cannot rescue an incorrect rear silhouette.

## Choreograph static action

For pose construction, shot selection, timing and the placement of individual
strokes, use [Drawing motion in action panels](drawing-motion.md) as part of
thumbnailing the exchange.

Plan the complete exchange before generating its first panel. Record starting stance, actor goals, approach route, range, support foot or contact surface, attack direction, defense, contact or miss, reaction, displacement, object loss, damage and resulting positions. Magical or superhuman events still need the story's own consistent causal rules.

Select the instants that make the mechanism readable. Not every phase needs its own panel, but an omitted phase must remain inferable without a contradictory jump. A compact sequence might use preparation, one contact image, a witness reaction and a wide consequence. A surprise attack may intentionally hide preparation; do not add a warning pose that defeats the written surprise.

Animation principles transfer selectively to still images: anticipation can make the next action intelligible; a clear silhouette supports staging; body arcs and trailing clothes imply direction; exaggeration can stress force. Comics choose moments and spatial intervals instead of displaying continuous frame timing. [Chris Hurtt, anticipation](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/anticipation-the-12-basic-principles-of-animation/), [Animation Mentor, twelve principles](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/tutorial-12-principles-of-animation/), accessed 2026-11-20.

Use effects with a specified purpose:

| Effect | What it should explain | What to verify |
|---|---|---|
| Directional speed strokes | Travel or limb trajectory | They agree with the actual motion vector |
| Radial focus strokes | A point of attention or sudden emphasis | Their convergence does not imply a false contact |
| Impact flash and debris | Contact, force and affected surface | Contact target and aftermath remain understandable |
| Curved motion trail | Swing, spin or arc | Origin, pivot and endpoint agree |
| Ghosted limb or prop | Multiple moments of rapid movement | Explicitly declared, visually differentiated, no accidental extra anatomy |
| Hair, cloth, breath or sweat | Secondary action or exertion | Cause, direction and character condition agree |

The historical study's spear exchange uses extreme impacts followed by a wider view of displacement and a dropped weapon. Its later miniature replay explains a retreating step. Use that separation of drama and explanation; do not duplicate the injury when showing the replay. A bright contact flash may cover the exact anatomy if attacker, target, vector and consequence remain clear.

## Use expressive modes deliberately

Declare the scope and purpose of a representation change. Chibi, simplified comedy, imagined figures, memories, symbolic backgrounds and explanatory miniatures need a visible entry and a planned return to the ordinary scene. Their logic must not silently replace literal world state.

Retain recognizable anchors when simplifying: hair shape, outfit silhouette, signature accessory, palette and attitude. Choose the project's own proportion and simplification rules. The artist TehMochie demonstrates several chibi constructions rather than one mandatory ratio. [Find Your Chibi Style](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/4808), accessed 2026-11-20.

Study-derived examples to support scoped QA exceptions:

- A maid's rapid arm echoes and spiral eyes can be intentional comic performance. Unexplained solid extra limbs still fail.
- A group of hypothetical enforcers belongs to a thought visualization; it does not add those people to the actual room.
- A miniature replay communicates a past mechanism; it does not advance present time or apply another attack.
- A deep expressive shadow over the eyes can signal mood; it does not necessarily move the physical sun.

Never use a broad label such as "stylized" to waive all identity, anatomy or continuity checks. State which feature changes, why it changes and what stays stable.

## Establish light, depth and style

Maintain a scene lighting plan with source position, direction, color, softness, occluders, time and important cast shadows. Distinguish a physical light change from an expressive graphic treatment. When the camera moves, compute which side of the character faces the same world light; do not keep a highlight on screen-left by habit.

Shading should describe the form and material: direct light, form shadow, cast shadow and reflected light have different roles. Preserve face and costume colors under plausible illumination instead of treating every lighting variation as an identity change. [Eridey, Guide to Drawing Shadows](https://www.clipstudio.net/how-to-draw/archives/163236), accessed 2026-11-20.

For drawn depth, specify overlap, size reduction, perspective convergence, detail hierarchy, contrast falloff and edge softness. Use selective focus sparingly when it improves the intended read. Do not blur essential landmarks into ambiguity or turn every background into photographic bokeh. Drawn focus can come from simpler shapes and lower contrast while preserving crisp line-art conventions.

Keep a small style specification: line weight behavior, shadow-edge treatment, rendering density, palette families, face construction, texture use, effects language and accepted exaggeration. It must describe visible properties rather than merely name an artist or genre. Compare later panels to both the original style references and accepted nearby panels; repeated copying of a drifting panel compounds the error.

## Review and repair

Review the proposed panel against three contexts: the correct resources, its approved beat and spatial plan, and neighboring accepted panels. Inspect the whole frame at reading size, then native-resolution crops of faces, hands, costume identifiers, prop grips and contact points. Also inspect the page or scroll thumbnail for hierarchy.

Classify each finding before repair: wrong identity, wrong speaker, impossible geography, incorrect action, unsupported emotion, anatomical defect, style drift, missing plot information, or composition/lettering collision. Record visible evidence and the exact affected region. Do not call a generated image perfect because an automated manifest validator passed.

Repair the smallest affected scope that preserves accepted content. If a local edit changes identity, lighting or body mechanics, review those dependent regions again. If the underlying plan is contradictory, fix the plan and regenerate affected panels instead of accumulating compensating edits. Stop promotion while critical findings remain unresolved.

## Scenario probes

Use these as decision tests and visual test briefs, not as claims of completed generation testing:

1. Two near-identical siblings argue beside different landmarks; a reverse view must retain identity, speaker and world position.
2. A smiling antagonist thinks an angry line; preserve the intended outward performance and thought ownership.
3. A dropped prop is picked up by another person; update owner, hand and every later appearance.
4. A fast impact hides contact; the preparation and consequence must make the mechanism understandable.
5. A miniature replay follows the fight; no current-state injury or movement is applied twice.
6. A comic motion echo appears; distinguish declared ghosts from an accidental extra arm.
7. A symbolic memory contains new people; they must not leak into the present scene.
8. When a study comes from another genre, use only the techniques the current brief calls for. Check the cast, props and setting against this project.
9. An extreme foreground hand preserves face likeness and attaches coherently to its own character.
10. A scene changes from noon to evening; accept a motivated lighting transition while preserving costume base colors and object identity.
