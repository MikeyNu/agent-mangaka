# Drawing black holes and spatial distortion

Start with the verb in the script. Containing a dark point in glass, holding a small one above a hand, consuming an object and opening a passage each require different visual evidence. Check the brief for which events actually happen, how long they last and what remains. A reference can supply the tilt of a band without supplying the spell, its colour or its consequences.

## Build the effect in three values

1. Draw the caster, environment and one recognizable straight contour first. Place the core in relation to something the reader can measure, such as a fingertip, a window frame or a skyline. Keep its centre quiet and dark. At tiny scale, a single dot and one bent reflection may carry the entire beat.
2. When the design calls for a luminous disk, sketch its plane as a tilted ellipse. Lay the near band across the dark silhouette, with visible dark space on either side of the crossing. Let the rim or far arc reappear beyond the silhouette. Taper the extensions as they turn away. Keep the dark centre visible at the intended reading size. A band that eclipses the centre can read as a laser through a glowing ball.
3. Bend a recognizable reflection, seam or edge as it approaches the core. Keep enough of the original contour visible farther away for the reader to recognize it. In a contained effect, draw through the transparent material first and mask the distortion to its interior. Give the glass edge and surface highlight their own intact silhouettes where the script keeps the enclosure whole.
4. Place short, uneven accents on the brightest portions of the rim. Where light reaches the scene, pick the facing plane of a knuckle, cheek, sleeve fold or railing. Keep its adjacent plane under the scene's ordinary light. Compare silhouette and band in grayscale before adding colour, sparks or bloom.

## Choose a shot by what it must prove

| Panel purpose | Drawing decision |
| --- | --- |
| A hand holds a compact core | Leave a sliver of air between fingertip and core if they are separate. Overlap a near finger only where depth requires it. Preserve the actor's hand anatomy, gaze and expression. |
| A large field changes the scene | Establish a landmark and the camera position. Carry the same landmark into the distorted view. Measure the visible core and the field's reach separately. A small core can command a large field if the script allows it. |
| A vortex gathers or turns | Follow one direction through two or three unequal arcs. Interrupt a rear arc behind a nearer form. A luminous spiral with a bright heart can teach how the eye travels. For a black-centred design, rebuild the value pattern around a dark core. |
| A core moves or consumes | Show its origin, then show it separated from the source. Use the same edge of the target before and after the crossing. Once the core goes out, let the missing or restored section carry precisely the consequence the script gives. |
| Space distorts without a visible core | Preserve fixed features on both sides of a displaced line or silhouette. Add a core, opening, debris or flash only when the scene calls for it. |

## Check the panel and the cut

Hide lettering, glow and particles. At final phone or print size, identify what is in front, what bends and where the centre is. If the band reads as a straight beam, turn its ends back into the orbital curve and vary the visible width. If the form reads as a portal, inspect the centre for an accidental view through to another space. If the surroundings read as smoke, replace a stray wisp with an identifiable bent contour. Across a cut, track the anchor, core diameter, disk plane, motion and last visible consequence in world space. A reverse camera angle can change the screen tilt of the same disk.
